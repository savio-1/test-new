# Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv (New York)

URL: https://cghnyc.com. Founded in 1957 by Ivan Chermayeff and Tom Geismar. Sagi Haviv became a partner in 2013. I reviewed the homepage logo grid and the U.S. Open, National Geographic, America250 and Pew Research Center case studies.

## Philosophy
"Creators of many of the world's most iconic and enduring brands… an independent design firm specializing in the development of trademarks and identity programs."

CGH works by **reduction**:
- a mark must be simple, appropriate and distinctive;
- it must work at any size and in one colour;
- it must last for decades.

Haviv's mantra is "the logo is not the brand". The mark is a signature, and it gets its meaning from the organisation's behaviour. The canon includes Chase, NBC, Mobil, PBS, National Geographic, Smithsonian and the U.S. Open.

## Typography
- The site uses **Camphor** (a humanist sans) at 34px, regular weight, -0.5px tracking, in sentence case. Captions are about 11px.
- The firm's own wordmark is set in small bold caps. Each "&" and partner name is a different primary: blue, red and green.
- Client wordmarks are mostly **custom-drawn sans or classic serif caps**: a widely letterspaced "HEARST", Apollo in spaced Roman caps, and an italic lowercase "us open".

## Layout & grid
- The homepage is a **3-column logo grid**. Tiles are 4:3 with about a 2% gutter and a 3% outer margin.
- **Each mark sits centred on its own flat brand colour**, or on white. The mark fills about 35–45% of the tile width.
- The caption sits below in small grey text: "Client – Logo, Identity Program".
- Case pages:
  - a 20px title above a 16:9 hero (a full-width photo with the mark overlaid);
  - a centred narrow text column (about 45% width, starting at 25%);
  - a full-bleed solid-colour band with the mark centred;
  - then in-situ photography, full-width or 2-up.

## Colour
The house is warm grey (#F5F5F5) with black text. Colour comes entirely from clients: saturated primaries (ultramarine, signal red, golden yellow) used as **large flat fields**.

## Imagery
- **In-situ proof**:
  - banners on lamp posts;
  - stadium jumbotrons;
  - signage on buildings;
  - a tote in black-on-black;
  - tablets held by hands.
- Merchandise close-ups (an engraved money clip, an embossed book spine).
- Big, crowded documentary photographs in which the mark appears naturally.

## Case-study presentation
1. Hero (mark on event photography).
2. A short history paragraph.
3. The **mark alone on a full-bleed brand-colour field**.
4. Rationale text.
5. Environmental banners.
6. Stadium or night in-situ shots.
7. Merchandise details.
8. Print and digital.
9. A related-logos grid.

## Signature moves
- The mark centred alone on a single flat colour at about 40% of the field.
- A logo-grid "wall of fame" in uniform tiles.
- Partner-name colour-coding.
- One-colour reduction tests (black, white and reversed).
- Wide-tracked uppercase serif or sans wordmarks.
- Geometric symbols built from circles and squares.
- The mark used as a supergraphic crop on banners.

## 3 notable projects
- **U.S. Open (2018):** the flaming ball distilled into a yellow swoosh with an italic lowercase "us open" on ultramarine.
- **America250 (2023):** a continuous red, white and blue ribbon forms "250", paired with a serif "AMERICA".
- **Pew Research Center:** a radial "illumination" symbol, a serif wordmark and a coded palette for research areas.
