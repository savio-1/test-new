# Dentsu (Tokyo / global): Dentsu Inc. design and Dentsu Creative

URLs: https://www.dentsu.com, https://www.dentsu.co.jp/en/ and https://www.dentsucreative.com. **Note:** dentsucreative.com is behind a Vercel bot checkpoint and returned "Failed to verify your browser" to headless Chromium. I captured dentsu.com (the group house style) and the dentsu.co.jp news. The design work comes from D&AD, Lürzer's Archive and V&A records. On 2 October 2026, Dentsu Inc. was named **D&AD Design Agency of the Year 2026**.

## Philosophy
- **Group:** "Innovating to Impact": an "integrated growth and transformation partner", founded in Tokyo in 1901.
- **Design:** Dentsu Inc.'s Tokyo design teams (creative director Yoshihiro Yagi and others) practise **Japanese graphic craft at mass scale**. They:
  - use public-participation systems (a stamp for every station);
  - reinterpret traditional forms (ukiyo-e, woodblock, hanko stamps);
  - give a single metaphor a visual form (the dandelion seed in "Peaceful Weapon").
- Dentsu Creative, created in 2022 from the merged creative networks, frames the brand as "east meets west".

## Typography
- **Group house style:** **Halcom** (a geometric-humanist sans).
  - Bold display at about 8% of width ("Innovating to Impact"), sentence case, tight leading.
  - Medium for card titles; uppercase 12px with 6px tracking for client eyebrows.
  - The "dentsu" wordmark is lowercase bold.
- **Japan design work:**
  - Japanese characters set large and vertical;
  - typographic systems built by teams of typographers (My Japan Railway credits several);
  - woodblock-style lettering.
- The Dentsu Creative "DC" mark is made of two facing semicircular brackets.

## Layout & grid
- **dentsu.com:** a 12-column grid with about 4.5% margins. Its moves:
  - a full-bleed hero photo (a Shibuya scramble crossing from above) with a big headline overlapping the image;
  - **an image overlapped by an offset navy text panel** (the image at 0–55%, the panel at 45–92%, dropped 15% lower);
  - a 3-column, edge-to-edge tile wall of work (4:3, no gutter) with a title and chevron at bottom-left;
  - an agency logo row.
- **Japan posters:** vertical B-format (1:1.414) with strong central symmetry and lots of empty ground.

## Colour
- **Group:** deep navy (#0A0A23-ish) and white with photographic colour.
- **Japan design:** traditional pigments (vermilion, indigo, sumi black) on off-white paper, and woodblock flat colour.

## Imagery
- Aerial city photography and people collaborating (corporate).
- **Woodblock and stamp-style illustration**.
- Pop ukiyo-e scenes (Seibu Railway manner posters).
- Botanical metaphors photographed or drawn (dandelion clocks).
- Gift-boxed packaging still-lifes.

## Signature moves
- A collectible icon per location (one woodblock-style stamp per station).
- A poster series in a strict template with only the illustration changing.
- Ukiyo-e flat colour applied to contemporary etiquette scenes.
- One natural metaphor carried across posters, film and exhibition décor.
- Corporate: a navy panel overlapping a photo, and a gutterless 3-up tile wall.

## 3 notable projects
- **My Japan Railway (JR Group):** a woodblock-style stamp designed for each station to collect and download. D&AD Black Pencil, Art Direction, 2024.
- **Seibu Railway Manner Posters (2017):** etiquette posters in pop ukiyo-e style. Held in the V&A collection.
- **Peaceful Weapon (2024):** the One Show Exhibition identity at the Ad Museum Tokyo. Dandelion seeds as a metaphor for how messages spread.
