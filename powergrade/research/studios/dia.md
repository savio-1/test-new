# DIA (New York / Chamonix)

URL: https://www.dia.studio. Founded in 2009 in SoHo by Mitch Paone (creative director, a jazz pianist who teaches at ECAL, HEAD, ZHdK and KABK) and Meg Donohoe (managing director). Sources: the homepage index and the Chaumont Biennale, Adidas London, Google Design Notes, Flodesk, Cohere North and MCA Denver pages. Many assets are MP4 loops, so frames were pulled with ffmpeg.

## Philosophy
- DIA calls itself a "design, research and innovation studio" that builds "kinetic identity systems, typographic systems, and generative tools".
- Its essays state the method: "Identity = Behavior", "Time Is the Material", "Time is Form, Form is Time".
- The studio says it develops tools that let it "relinquish manual control and let a system generate the unexpected" (Chaumont).

## Typography
- The site is set only in **JJannon** (Optimo), 16px, in black and 50% grey.
- Client work runs on grotesks and custom faces pushed through code:
  - letters are stretched, squeezed, rotated 90° and repeated as vertical columns ("LONDON" and "ALIVE" walls at Adidas);
  - type is wrapped onto 3D surfaces in video (MCA Denver).
- For Flodesk, a custom sans and serif were drawn with François Rappo (Optimo).

## Layout & grid
- The homepage is a scatter of tiny thumbnails on a vast white field.
- Case pages are archival:
  - a top bar reading "DIA: Project", with the project name in grey and "[ Next ][ Close ]" in brackets;
  - a uniform 4-column grid of 16:10 tiles with about 6px gutters;
  - one text band underneath, with the description on the left half and Credits and Year on the right half.

## Colour
- The frame is black on white.
- Projects are mostly monochrome with one accent: Chaumont meme-mosaics, Flodesk warm orange and navy, Cohere North pale mint with forest green.

## Imagery
- Motion stills and loops: type on retail LED walls and in windows, generative contour lines, distorted UI.
- In-situ documentary photography of posters on French street furniture, totes on people and book spreads on concrete.

## Case-study presentation
- The order is: grid of video loops (identity animation) → printed matter → posters in situ → merchandise (tote, enamel pin, stickers) → publication spreads → text.
- Every tile has the same size, so motion and print carry equal weight.

## Signature moves
1. The wordmark as a time-based behaviour, not a static lockup.
2. Letters rotated into vertical columns and repeated as a wall.
3. Elastic letter widths that follow a wave function.
4. Characters replaced by image or meme mosaics.
5. Generative line fields (contours, Voronoi) as brand texture.
6. Type mapped onto real architecture in video.
7. Uniform thumbnail grids for documentation.

## Notable projects
- **Adidas London flagship (2019).** A generative content system with FIELD. Visuals never repeat during a visit, across the LED walls, window signage and storefront.
- **Chaumont Biennale "Viral" (2021).** A custom tool that generates kinetic type from memes, used across posters, tote, pins and catalogue.
- **Flodesk (2023).** A full rebrand with a custom typeface, a motion system and product integration. Packaging, caps and billboards.
