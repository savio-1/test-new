# NOT Wieden+Kennedy (London)

URL: https://notwk.london. **Verified:** this is the standalone branding and design studio of Wieden+Kennedy London, launched in January 2023. Its creative director is Adam Rix and its brand director is Anika Ramani. It sits at 16 Hanbury St, E1, the W+K London address.

## Philosophy
"The world needs another branding and design agency. Said no-one ever." NOT takes Dan Wieden's "Move me, dude" and goes "beyond the nice, the polite, the rational and respectful – to surprise, inspire and incite. To make us look at something, then look again." It rejects a house style and treats identity as reactive and generative. Brands should behave like culture, not like rulebooks.

## Typography
- The site is set in Inter Light (100) at −0.4px for UI and Xanh Mono for the footer.
- The NOT logotype is a generative, inflated 3D script built from stacked icon "brushes". Each letter is a tube made of hundreds of repeated emoji or objects (hearts, eyeballs, liquorice). It sits next to a thin grotesk "Wieden+Kennedy" set in two lines with a superscript "+".
- In client work the type is bold and familiar, then subverted: Cooper Black for Dark Arts Coffee, and tightly kerned motorsport sans throughout the F1 guidelines in the reel.

## Layout & grid
- The website is a single centred column: logo at 40–55% of the width, a centred manifesto at 10px, and a full-viewport work reel that crossfades below.
- The work reel shows one image per screen, full-bleed or as a centred 2–3-up. Examples: posters in a row on black, a 9 × 9 grid of brand-guideline pages, a wall of repeated type.
- There are no case-study pages. The studio's work is shown as a reel and through press.

## Colour
- The studio's own colours are pastel gradient grounds (pink #F07CF5 to cyan #3BB8D9 to grey) and black/white chrome.
- Client palettes run loud: black-on-black with pop-art card inserts for Dark Arts, red/blue/acid yellow sports posters.

## Signature moves
- Generative wordmarks drawn with an "icon brush" (the NOT Machine): 3D tubes, wiggle and bounce motion.
- One brand, many logos: the W+K logo reinterpreted by a baker, a barber, a letterpress printer and school kids.
- A collectible system: a fixed black pack plus an ever-changing artist card in a window.
- A familiar retro face (Cooper Black) used with deadpan dark humour ("Joyful Nihilism").
- Repetition walls: one phrase repeated to fill a poster.
- A pastel gradient ground behind a glossy 3D object.

## 3 notable projects
- **NOT W+K identity and the NOT Machine (2023):** a public tool that turns any image or icon into an animated custom wordmark.
- **Dark Arts Coffee (2024; D&AD 2025):** "Joyful Nihilism". Cooper Black, a cracked smiley mark, black windowed boxes, and 50+ collectible artist cards across 100+ coffees.
- **W+K London logo project (2025):** an evolving series of makers each reinterpreting the W+K logo.
