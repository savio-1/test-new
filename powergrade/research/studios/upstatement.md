# Upstatement (Boston / New York)

URL: https://upstatement.com. Founded in 2008. Now part of kyu, with 40+ people across Boston, NYC, LA, DC, London and Barcelona. Sources: the homepage and the Nike, Etsy, MIT.edu and Harvard Innovation Labs case studies.

## Philosophy
- The studio describes itself with "Brand Through Digital™": "a global design and technology studio with journalistic roots. We believe all brands are digital, and that systems can tell stories."
- It was born in the newsrooms of the Boston Globe and the NYT.
- It designs the website and digital system first and then extends outward, which reverses the usual brand-book-first order.

## Typography
- The site pairs **Diatype** (Dinamo) for UI and body (20px, weights 380/700) with **Rizoma**, a light, high-contrast editorial serif.
- Rizoma is used for display: "Brand Through Digital" at about 40px, client names, and the footer "Think we can help? Let's talk."
- Client lists are set as big underlined serif links.
- Case titles are in serif sentence case. The subtitles are small bold sans.
- Both faces are untracked, and nothing is set in uppercase.

## Layout & grid
- Case openers follow an editorial template:
  - left column (4–34% of width): serif title, bold sub-deck, a hairline rule, a 9-line body, then "What We Did / What We Made" as two small lists;
  - right half: a hero photo.
- Deeper in the page, images float like a magazine collage on black. They come in very different sizes (7–30% of width), offset vertically, with no shared baselines.
- Sparse white dot-grid markers punctuate the empty space.
- Centred single paragraphs separate the collage sections.

## Colour
- The studio frame is pure black with white type.
- Case pages adopt the client's ground: warm stone (#E9E7E2) for Harvard, white for Etsy, black for Nike and MIT.
- Accents come from the client, such as Etsy orange and Nike red.

## Imagery
- Phones and laptops shown in real environments. For MIT, a phone sits in a magenta grow-lab.
- Campaign photography cut into a grid of thumbnails.
- Product cutouts with no frame.
- Data-viz diagrams such as user-journey curves.
- Award badges, for example a Webby nominee card.

## Case-study presentation
The usual order is: editorial opener → problem paragraph → a wall of campaign assets → collage of touchpoints (email, PDP, social) → UI phone stacks → typography framework slide → toolkit overview ("bird's-eye view" of all components) → testimonial quote → "Up Next".

## Signature moves
1. A serif plus grotesk pairing, both untracked, all in sentence case.
2. A floating collage of mixed sizes on black, with dot markers.
3. The "What We Did / What We Made" two-list metadata block.
4. UI shown in situ inside real places.
5. A bird's-eye overview of the whole toolkit as a final proof.
6. Underlined serif client names used as a typographic list.

## Notable projects
- **Boston Globe (2011).** An early mainstream responsive news site, and the studio's origin story.
- **MIT.edu (c. 2018).** A homepage made of fragmented "MIT" bars that assemble, plus smart search.
- **Etsy (c. 2025, with SYLVAIN).** "Keep Commerce Human": a product brand toolkit built around a square metaphor, used in the buyer app and web.
